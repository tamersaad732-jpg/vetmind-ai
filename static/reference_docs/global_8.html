Chapter 1: Problem-Oriented Diagnostic Approach to Equine Acute Colic

Systematic Colic Workup:

1. Pain Level Evaluation: Mild intermittent pawing vs. violent rolling refractory to analgesics (surgical emergency).

2. Nasogastric Intubation: Mandatory in all colic cases before administering oral medications. Measure spontaneous gastric reflux; presence of > 2 liters of foul net reflux indicates proximal enteritis or small intestinal obstruction.

3. Rectal Palpation: Systematically examine caudal border of spleen, left kidney, nephrosplenic space, pelvic flexure, cecum, and mesenteric root.

4. Abdominocentesis: Normal peritoneal fluid is clear straw-colored, total protein < 2.0 g/dL, nucleated cells < 5,000/mcL, and lactate < 2.0 mmol/L. High lactate (> 4.0 mmol/L) indicates bowel ischemia and necrosis requiring immediate laparotomy.
