Chapter 1: Multi-System Physical Examination & Clinical Triage Standards

Systematic Physical Assessment Hierarchy:

1. Distance Examination: Mentation, respiratory pattern, posture, gait, body condition score (BCS 1-9).

2. Vital Signs & Telemetry:

  • Temperature, Pulse, Respiration (TPR) normal baseline:
  • Canine: T 38.3-39.2 C | HR 70-140 bpm | RR 18-34 bpm
  • Feline: T 38.0-39.2 C | HR 140-220 bpm | RR 20-30 bpm
  • Equine: T 37.2-38.3 C | HR 28-44 bpm | RR 8-16 bpm
  • Bovine: T 38.0-39.3 C | HR 48-84 bpm | RR 24-36 bpm

    • 3. Mucous Membrane & Perfusion Indicators:

  • Capillary Refill Time (CRT): Normal < 2.0 seconds. Prolonged (> 2s) indicates peripheral vasoconstriction or hypovolemic shock.
  • Pale mucous membranes indicate severe anemia or hypovolemia.
  • Brick-red / injected membranes indicate endotoxemic septic shock or severe vasodilation.
  • Icteric (yellow) membranes indicate pre-hepatic hemolysis, hepatic insufficiency, or post-hepatic biliary stasis.

    • Chapter 2: Emergency Fluid Resuscitation Therapy

    Standard Veterinary Fluid Calculations:

    1. Total Fluid Requirement = Deficit Volume + Daily Maintenance + Ongoing Losses.

  • Deficit Volume (mL) = Body Weight (kg) x % Dehydration x 10.
  • Maintenance Volumes:
  • Adult Canine: 60 mL/kg/day (or 132 x BW^0.75 mL/day).
  • Adult Feline: 40-50 mL/kg/day.
  • Large Animals (Equine / Bovine): 50-60 mL/kg/day.

    • 2. Emergency Shock Dosing (Balanced Isotonic Crystalloids: LRS / Normosol-R):

  • Canine Shock Dose: 80-90 mL/kg IV (give 25% of total bolus over 15 minutes, then reassess perfusion targets).
  • Feline Shock Dose: 40-60 mL/kg IV (give 10-15 mL/kg aliquots slowly; cats are highly susceptible to volume overload and pulmonary edema).
  • Equine Shock Dose: 60-90 mL/kg IV via dual 10-gauge catheters.

    • Chapter 3: Clinical Toxicology & Decontamination Protocols

    Acute Ingestion Toxin Management:

    1. Induction of Emesis:

  • Canine: Apomorphine hydrochloride at 0.03 mg/kg IV or subconjunctival tablet (0.25 mg dissolved in saline into conjunctival sac; rinse upon emesis). Hydrogen peroxide 3% (1-2 mL/kg PO, max 45 mL).
  • Feline: Dexmedetomidine at 7 mcg/kg IM (reverse with Atipamezole once vomiting occurs). Do not administer Apomorphine to felines (ineffective and causes CNS depression).

    • 2. Gastrointestinal Adsorption:

  • Activated Charcoal (with or without Sorbitol): 1 to 4 g/kg PO as a 10% slurry. Repeat q4-6h at 0.5 g/kg without cathartic for enterohepatically recirculated toxins.
